Inside a mind

What the agent is given is layered, from what never changes to what changes every day. Beliefs are not facts: they come from experience, and they can be out of date or wrong.

How it works, in full →

  1. IdentityPersonality (five traits), values, one life aspiration. Stable for life.
  2. BeliefsWhere food is, who can be trusted, whose land this is. Learned, and fallible.
  3. TodayWhat they see, how hungry and tired they are, who is near, what was offered.
  4. ActionGather, eat, rest, share, court, build, claim, trade, teach, take, fight.

Nothing is scripted

There is no code for markets, chiefs or feuds. There are agents with needs, a world with rules, and time. What you see in a run comes out of that.

  • Sharing. Food flows from those with plenty to those without, most readily between kin and friends, and between people who have given before.
  • Ownership. People claim the land around their homes. Owners ward off trespassers; most intruders back off, and a desperate few don't.
  • Conflict. When food runs short and trust runs low, some take by force what they can't find, and those they wrong remember.
  • Families. Courtships turn into bonds, bonds into children. In a big enough world, generations follow one another.
  • Hard years. Droughts and storms come without warning; illness passes between neighbours. Settlements rise, decay and are rebuilt.

Two kinds of mind

The intelligence can come from either of two places. Either way, the world never chooses for anyone.
